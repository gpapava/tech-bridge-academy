Project Work is a teaching strategy based on the idea that learning is activated and developed effectively when students face situations to resolve. Students learn while developing and elaborating projects autonomously — whether producing a process or developing a product — and in this generative work they must integrate the experiences and competences they have acquired.
Authentic
Connected to reality and to students' interests and curiosities — not a theoretical exercise.
Complex enough
Challenging enough for students to feel stretched by the task, but not so difficult as to overwhelm them.
Feasible
Built on the overall capabilities students already possess — both content knowledge and project management skills.
Commissioned work
Configured as a commission co-designed by school and company together as joint clients.
Individual or group
Can be carried out individually or in small, mixed groups. Group work adds relational competences and requires more time.
Intrinsically motivating
Exploits the appeal of working creatively and autonomously to produce something real — indirect motivation for learning.

Like all work-based learning devices, Project Work is structured in five phases. School and company work at time T to design the device; students begin their own process at time T+1 when they receive the commission.
School / VET centre
  • Define the Project Work's learning objectives in line with the curriculum exit profile
  • Co-design the commission with the company
  • Prepare the project brief and planning documents
  • Define preparatory or follow-up activities
  • Prepare monitoring and assessment tools
  • Support students during the delivery phase
  • Evaluate product and learning process together with the company
Company
  • Define a real need and co-design the commission with the school
  • Designate a company tutor
  • Provide data, technical documentation, and context
  • If the PW is hosted at the company: provide workspace, equipment, and safety compliance
  • Support and accompany students during delivery
  • Provide feedback and evaluate the outputs jointly with the school
Students
  • Organise themselves to respond to the commission
  • Develop an initial project concept
  • Plan the activities to be carried out
  • Monitor the progress of each activity
  • Deliver the completed product
  • Present outcomes to all parties involved in assessment
  • Self-assess the experience
Assessment should consider both the product and the learning process. The assessment rubric is the primary tool: it makes evaluation criteria explicit and creates a shared language between school and company. Four proficiency levels are used: not achieved — basic — intermediate — advanced.
Coherence with the commission
Does the solution respond to the needs and requirements stated in the commission? Does it show understanding of the problem?
Technical quality
Is the solution technically plausible, feasible, and attentive to assembly, safety, and maintenance?
Documentation
Are the technical deliverables complete, clear, and consistent? Is the technical report adequate and comprehensible?
Application of competences
Are technical and professional competences applied correctly, with autonomy and precision across the different dimensions of the problem?
Organisation and collaboration
Does the group work autonomously? Are roles distributed? Is the process documented and deadlines respected?
Presentation and argumentation
Is the final presentation clear, technical, and convincing? Can the group respond pertinently to the client's questions?
Critical reflection
Does the group analyse the process critically, motivate their choices, and propose realistic future developments?

Eight key principles to keep in mind when designing and running a Project Work — from the initial brief to the final transfer of learning.
1
Start from a real need — the commission must connect to an authentic professional context
2
Design jointly — school and company co-design the commission and the learning pathway
3
Make learning outcomes explicit — define expected competences before students begin
4
Support students with progressive tools — briefs, planning sheets, intermediate checkpoints
5
Monitor throughout the process — do not wait for the final delivery to identify problems
6
Assess both process and product — the how matters as much as the what
7
Promote self-assessment — students should reflect on their own learning journey
8
Foster transfer — connect the PW experience back to classroom learning and future contexts
